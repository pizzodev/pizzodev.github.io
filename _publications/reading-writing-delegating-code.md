Code comprehension relies heavily on general-purpose problem-solving systems, not the same brain regions used for language.

Research on the generation and testing effects suggests that actively producing and recalling information strengthens learning more than passive reading-but these studies were not about coding.

AI agents can substantially improve coding productivity, especially for novices and short, well-defined tasks, though results vary for experienced developers and longer tasks.

A 2026 randomized trial found that developers who fully delegated unfamiliar coding tasks to AI scored 17% lower on later tests of understanding, code reading, and debugging.

Conversational AI use—asking why, requesting explanations, and working through errors—largely preserved learning while retaining much of AI's productivity benefit.

Practical approach: attempt first, ask conceptual questions, explain solutions back, experience some debugging, and periodically solve old problems without AI.

The takeaway isn't “use AI less”; it's “use AI as a learning partner, not a vending machine.”
